TCFD Scenario Analysis — How to Do It Properly

Scenario analysis is the part of climate reporting most companies fake and assurers increasingly test. This guide shows how to pick a defensible scenario set, quantify what matters and write the resilience statement IFRS S2 paragraph 22 requires.

Key facts — verified & sourced

TCFD scenario analysis expectations

  • TCFD (and IFRS S2 para 22) expect scenario analysis proportional to the entity's exposure, including at least one ≤2°C scenario, with disclosure of assumptions, time horizons and resilience conclusions.
  • Good practice pairs one orderly, one disorderly and one hot-house scenario, and quantifies impact on revenue, costs, and asset values by segment.

Source: fsb-tcfd.org · reviewed 2026-07

NGFS scenario families

  • NGFS scenarios group into Orderly (Net Zero 2050, Below 2°C), Disorderly (Delayed Transition), Hot House World (Current Policies, NDCs), and Too-Little-Too-Late (Fragmented World).
  • Net Zero 2050 assumes immediate coordinated policy, ~1.4–1.5°C in 2100, high transition risk but low physical risk; Current Policies reaches ~3°C with severe physical risk.
  • NGFS Phase V (Nov 2024) updated damage functions — chronic physical damages materially higher than earlier phases.

Source: ngfs.net · reviewed 2026-07

Carbon price trajectories by scenario

  • In NGFS Net Zero 2050, modelled global carbon prices reach roughly US$100–200/tCO2 by 2030 and several hundred by 2050 (model-dependent).
  • Delayed Transition scenarios show low prices this decade then a sharp spike after 2030 — the signature 'disorderly' shock to test in stress scenarios.
  • Use scenario carbon prices as shadow prices on Scope 1+2 to estimate EBITDA-at-risk.

Source: ngfs.net · reviewed 2026-07

Frequently asked questions

How many scenarios do I need?

At least one aligned with ≤2°C. Good practice pairs three: an orderly transition, a disorderly one, and a hot-house physical-risk scenario.

Which scenarios should financial institutions use?

NGFS scenarios are the supervisory default — they cover orderly (Net Zero 2050), disorderly (Delayed Transition) and hot-house (Current Policies) pathways with published carbon-price and damage variables.

What goes in the resilience conclusion?

For each scenario: the material impacts on revenue, costs and assets by segment and time horizon, and what strategy changes (capex, hedging, exit) follow.
