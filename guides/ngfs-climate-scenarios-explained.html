NGFS Climate Scenarios Explained — Net Zero to Hot House

The Network for Greening the Financial System scenarios are the shared language of climate risk for central banks, supervisors and reporters. This guide decodes the families and the variables you should actually pull out of them.

Key facts — verified & sourced

NGFS scenario families

  • NGFS scenarios group into Orderly (Net Zero 2050, Below 2°C), Disorderly (Delayed Transition), Hot House World (Current Policies, NDCs), and Too-Little-Too-Late (Fragmented World).
  • Net Zero 2050 assumes immediate coordinated policy, ~1.4–1.5°C in 2100, high transition risk but low physical risk; Current Policies reaches ~3°C with severe physical risk.
  • NGFS Phase V (Nov 2024) updated damage functions — chronic physical damages materially higher than earlier phases.

Source: ngfs.net · reviewed 2026-07

Carbon price trajectories by scenario

  • In NGFS Net Zero 2050, modelled global carbon prices reach roughly US$100–200/tCO2 by 2030 and several hundred by 2050 (model-dependent).
  • Delayed Transition scenarios show low prices this decade then a sharp spike after 2030 — the signature 'disorderly' shock to test in stress scenarios.
  • Use scenario carbon prices as shadow prices on Scope 1+2 to estimate EBITDA-at-risk.

Source: ngfs.net · reviewed 2026-07

Physical risk hazard set (IPCC AR6)

  • Acute hazards: riverine/coastal flood, tropical cyclone, heatwave, wildfire, drought; chronic: sea-level rise, water stress, mean temperature rise, precipitation shift.
  • AR6 projects sea-level rise of ~0.28–0.55 m by 2100 under low emissions and ~0.63–1.01 m under very high emissions.
  • South Asia is a flagged hotspot for compounding heat-humidity risk; wet-bulb thresholds constrain outdoor labour productivity.

Source: ipcc.ch · reviewed 2026-07

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between orderly and disorderly?

Orderly scenarios assume policy starts now and tightens smoothly; disorderly ones assume delay followed by an abrupt correction — same destination, much higher transition cost.

What carbon price should I test?

Under Net Zero 2050, modelled prices reach roughly US$100–200/tCO2 by 2030 — apply them as shadow prices to Scope 1+2 to estimate earnings at risk.

Which scenario has the worst physical risk?

Current Policies (hot-house): ~3°C warming by 2100, with chronic damages revised sharply upward in NGFS Phase V.
