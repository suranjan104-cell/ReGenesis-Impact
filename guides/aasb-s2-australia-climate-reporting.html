AASB S2 — Australia’s Mandatory Climate Reporting Explained

Australia’s Treasury Laws Amendment (Financial Market Infrastructure) Act made climate reporting mandatory, phased by entity size from 1 January 2025. Here is who reports when, and what auditors will expect.

Key facts — verified & sourced

AASB S2 Australian phase-in groups

  • Group 1 (two of: revenue ≥ A$500m, assets ≥ A$1bn, employees ≥ 500) reports for financial years starting 1 January 2025.
  • Group 2 (two of: A$200m revenue, A$500m assets, 250 employees) starts FYs from 1 July 2026; Group 3 (A$50m revenue, A$25m assets, 100 employees) from 1 July 2027.
  • Scope 3, scenario analysis and transition plan statements have a 3-year 'protected statements' regime — only ASIC can take action in that window.

Source: treasury.gov.au · reviewed 2026-07

Australia assurance phasing (AUASB)

  • AUASB's phased approach: year-one assurance covers governance disclosures and Scope 1–2 (limited), expanding across all climate disclosures, reaching reasonable assurance for FYs commencing from 1 July 2030.
  • The auditor of the financial report is expected to conduct the sustainability assurance for consistency of materiality and group scoping.

Source: auasb.gov.au · reviewed 2026-07

APRA CPS 229 climate risk expectations

  • APRA's prudential guidance (CPG 229) expects regulated entities to embed climate risk in governance, risk appetite and scenario analysis; the Climate Vulnerability Assessment ran with the five largest banks.
  • AASB S2 reporting and APRA expectations interlock: scenario analysis capabilities built for CVA feed directly into mandatory disclosure.

Source: apra.gov.au · reviewed 2026-07

Frequently asked questions

Which group is my company in?

You meet a group if you satisfy any two of its three thresholds. Group 1: revenue ≥ A$500m, assets ≥ A$1bn, employees ≥ 500. Group 2: A$200m / A$500m / 250. Group 3: A$50m / A$25m / 100.

What are protected statements?

Scope 3, scenario analysis and transition plan statements get a 3-year regime where only ASIC can take action — private litigation is barred for those disclosures.

When is reasonable assurance required?

Assurance phases from limited (governance + Scope 1–2 first) to reasonable assurance over all climate disclosures for financial years commencing 1 July 2030.
