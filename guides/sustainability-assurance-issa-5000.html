Sustainability Assurance & ISSA 5000 — Getting Audit-Ready

Assurance is where sustainability reporting gets real: numbers must trace to evidence. With ISSA 5000 effective for periods from December 2026 and mandates phasing in across AU, IN and SG, this guide covers what practitioners test and how to be ready.

Key facts — verified & sourced

ISSA 5000 — the new umbrella standard

  • ISSA 5000 (approved by IAASB September 2024) is the comprehensive standard for sustainability assurance, applying to any framework (ISSB, GRI, ESRS) and both limited and reasonable assurance.
  • It is effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026, replacing reliance on ISAE 3000/3410 for sustainability engagements.
  • It is profession-agnostic — usable by audit firms and other assurance practitioners.

Source: iaasb.org · reviewed 2026-07

Limited vs reasonable assurance

  • Limited assurance: negative-form conclusion ('nothing came to our attention'), evidence mainly inquiry + analytical procedures; cost and effort materially lower.
  • Reasonable assurance: positive opinion ('fairly stated'), audit-equivalent evidence — walkthroughs, controls testing, substantive detail testing, recalculation.
  • Most regimes phase limited → reasonable (Australia reaches reasonable assurance for climate disclosures for FYs from 1 July 2030).

Source: iaasb.org · reviewed 2026-07

Top recurring assurance findings

  • Most common findings across GHG engagements: outdated emission factors, incomplete entity/facility coverage, unit conversion errors (GJ/MWh/kWh), estimation presented as measurement, market-based Scope 2 without valid certificates, and Scope 3 category omissions without justification.
  • Remediating before fieldwork is far cheaper than post-finding restatement — a readiness scan against these six items is standard practice.

Source: iaasb.org · reviewed 2026-07

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between limited and reasonable assurance?

Limited assurance gives a negative-form conclusion from inquiry and analytics; reasonable assurance is audit-grade — controls testing, substantive detail and a positive opinion. Cost and evidence demands differ accordingly.

What is ISSA 5000?

The IAASB’s comprehensive sustainability assurance standard (approved September 2024, effective for periods from 15 December 2026), applying to any framework and both assurance levels.

What are the most common findings?

Stale emission factors, unit conversion errors, incomplete entity coverage, estimation presented as measurement, and market-based Scope 2 claims without certificates.
