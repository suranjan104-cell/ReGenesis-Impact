Carbon Credits Buyer’s Guide — Quality, Prices and Claims

The voluntary carbon market rewards buyers who understand quality and punishes those who don’t — regulators have pursued greenwashing claims built on weak offsets. This guide covers the quality tests, realistic price ranges and how credits fit a credible net-zero strategy.

Key facts — verified & sourced

Credit quality fundamentals

  • A high-quality credit demonstrates additionality (wouldn't happen without credit revenue), permanence (reversal risk managed via buffer pools), no leakage (emissions don't shift elsewhere), and robust MRV.
  • ICVCM's Core Carbon Principles (2023) are the leading quality benchmark; CCP-labelled methodologies signal integrity to buyers.
  • Avoidance/reduction credits (e.g. REDD+, cookstoves) trade at a discount to durable removals (DAC, biochar, mineralisation).

Source: icvcm.org · reviewed 2026-07

Price benchmarks by category

  • Indicative voluntary-market ranges (2024–25): nature avoidance (REDD+) ~US$3–10/t, cookstoves ~US$5–15/t, ARR reforestation ~US$15–40/t, blue carbon premium ~US$20–40/t, durable removals US$100–600+/t.
  • Prices vary sharply with vintage, rating-agency scores (BeZero, Sylvera) and co-benefits — always quote a range, never a point price.

Source: ecosystemmarketplace.com · reviewed 2026-07

Using credits in net-zero claims

  • SBTi's Net-Zero Standard restricts credits to neutralising residual emissions (≤5–10%) at net-zero, not substituting for near-term abatement; interim use is 'beyond value chain mitigation'.
  • The mitigation hierarchy: reduce first, then remove, then offset — regulators (ACCC, CMA) have pursued greenwashing claims built on low-quality offsets.
  • IFRS S2 para 36(e) requires disclosing planned carbon-credit use: volume, type (reduction vs removal), registry and quality assumptions.

Source: sciencebasedtargets.org · reviewed 2026-07

Frequently asked questions

What makes a credit high quality?

Demonstrated additionality, managed permanence (buffer pools), no leakage, robust MRV, and ideally an ICVCM Core Carbon Principles label.

How much should credits cost?

Ranges vary hugely by type: REDD+ avoidance ~US$3–10/t, reforestation ~US$15–40/t, blue carbon ~US$20–40/t, durable removals US$100–600+/t. A too-cheap credit is a red flag.

Can I use credits to claim net zero?

Only for residual emissions (5–10%) after deep reduction under SBTi’s Net-Zero Standard — and IFRS S2 requires disclosing your planned credit use and quality assumptions.
