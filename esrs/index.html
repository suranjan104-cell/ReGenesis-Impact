What it does

01 · Scope

Are you caught?

The Omnibus made the EU test an AND, not an either/or: over 1,000 employees and over €450mn net turnover. Non-EU parent groups are tested on EU-generated turnover.

02 · Materiality

Double, not financial

A topic counts if it is material to the world or to the company. An IFRS S2 assessment is never sufficient evidence for an ESRS omission.

03 · Disclosures

65 requirements, tracked

Enumerated across ten standards and filtered by your materiality assessment, so you see what you file rather than the whole of Set 1.

04 · Taxonomy

Article 8 alignment

Turnover, CapEx and OpEx computed over entered denominators, through all four gates, with the 10% materiality floor applied per KPI independently.

05 · Tagging

Where the mandate stands

Mandatory XBRL tagging is suspended by Directive (EU) 2026/470. We say so, and score whether your statement could be tagged when it resumes.

06 · Interop

IFRS S2 carry-across

Eleven mappings from the joint IFRS Foundation and EFRAG guidance, so a company already reporting under IFRS S2 or AASB S2 does not rebuild its climate disclosure.

Why the numbers can be trusted

Every emission factor the calculators apply is published in a public register with its value, source, vintage and confidence grade — including the four we cannot source, which warn you at the point of entry and are named in the assurance pack. The GHG inventory is checked against twelve GHG Protocol Corporate Standard conditions and presented a second way under the six categories of ISO 14064-1:2018, reconciled to the scope view.

The ESRS and standards data are graded medium confidence, not high. EUR-Lex, EFRAG, ESMA, ghgprotocol.org and iso.org are unreachable from our build environment, so the thresholds and requirement names rest on professional-firm summaries. That is stated in the product, on every tab, rather than buried here.

Six worked cases

Each one is a fictional entity built to make a single rule visible, and each is loadable in the tool — the figures below are what the calculation actually returns, not a description of what it would return. The entities are invented on purpose: a worked example attributed to a real company is a fabricated record.

Nordhavn Marine Systems

Industrial manufacturing · Denmark

Shows: The Omnibus scope test is an AND. Missing either limb puts you out.

The mistake it exposes: Reading 1,200 employees and assuming scope, without checking turnover.

Result: Below the thresholds — 1,200 staff clears the employee test, but €380mn does not clear €450mn, and both are required.

The most common scoping error we see. An entity over the staff limb often assumes it is caught and starts a programme it does not yet owe. It may still be asked for the data by an in-scope customer or lender, which is a different conversation.

Kestrel Pacific Holdings

Diversified holdings · Singapore parent

Shows: A non-EU parent is tested on EU-generated turnover alone, not on group size.

The mistake it exposes: Assuming a non-EU headquarters puts the group outside the CSRD.

Result: In scope — €620mn of EU-generated turnover clears the €450mn non-EU group threshold. Headcount and global turnover are not the test.

Group size is irrelevant here. A large non-EU group with modest EU trade can be out; a mid-sized one with a strong European book can be in.

Rheinwerk Energie

Renewable generation · Germany

Shows: Minimum safeguards are an entity-level gate. Failing them zeroes alignment across all three KPIs, whatever the screening criteria say.

The mistake it exposes: Reporting high alignment from strong technical screening while the safeguards assessment is still outstanding.

Result: 0% aligned on every KPI despite 75% of turnover sitting in activities that contribute and do no harm — because minimum safeguards are unmet.

Turn safeguards on in the tool and the turnover KPI moves to 75%. That single switch is the difference between a green report and a zero, which is why it is an entity-level assessment done once rather than an afterthought.

Lys Grocers Group

Food retail · Belgium

Shows: The 10% materiality floor is applied to each KPI independently, so the same activity can be excluded from one and assessed in another.

The mistake it exposes: Applying one materiality decision across all three KPIs.

Result: Turnover reads 0% aligned — the fleet is 4.3% of turnover and falls under the 10% floor, so it is never assessed there. The same renovation is 37% of CapEx and is fully assessed in that KPI, where it drives alignment.

This is why CapEx alignment usually runs ahead of turnover alignment in a transitioning business: the spend to become aligned counts before the revenue does.

Aventine Asset Partners

Asset management · Luxembourg

Shows: The OpEx KPI can be omitted where it is not material to the business model — you disclose the total and why, not a computed ratio.

The mistake it exposes: Computing a meaningless OpEx ratio because the template has a box for it.

Result: OpEx reported as exempt with its total stated; turnover and CapEx computed normally.

Qualifying OpEx is a narrow definition — research, renovation, short leases, maintenance — not total operating cost. For a services business it is often immaterial, and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/73 lets you say so.

Corrib Data Centres

Digital infrastructure · Ireland

Shows: An IFRS S2 reporter arriving at CSRD carries the climate inventory across, but not the materiality assessment.

The mistake it exposes: Treating an existing ISSB materiality conclusion as evidence for omitting an ESRS topical standard.

Result: In scope, with E1-6 reading its evidence straight from the GHG inventory already built for IFRS S2 — but E3 water and E5 circularity are new, because impact materiality reaches them and financial materiality did not.

A data centre is the clearest case: water for cooling and equipment circularity are rarely financially material and are frequently impact-material. That asymmetry is the whole reason double materiality exists.

Questions

Am I in scope for the CSRD after the Omnibus?

An EU company is in scope only if it exceeds both 1,000 employees and €450mn net turnover. The Omnibus made this an AND test, which took a large number of previously in-scope companies out. Non-EU parent groups are tested on EU-generated turnover above €450mn.

Which ESRS apply to a 2026 financial year?

ESRS Set 1, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772. The revised simplified standards were adopted on 3 July 2026 and cut mandatory datapoints by 61%, but they apply to financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027 — so a FY2026 report is prepared under Set 1.

How are the EU Taxonomy Article 8 KPIs calculated?

Three KPIs — turnover, CapEx and OpEx — each an aligned numerator over its own denominator. Alignment needs four gates: eligible, substantial contribution to one of the six environmental objectives, do no significant harm to the other five, and entity-level minimum safeguards. Safeguards are assessed once and applied to everything, so failing them zeroes alignment across all three KPIs.

Do I have to report the OpEx KPI?

Not necessarily. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/73 allows the OpEx KPI to be omitted where it is not material to the business model, disclosing only its total value and why. The same regulation lets you skip assessing any activity below 10% of a KPI, applied to each KPI independently.

When does XBRL tagging become mandatory?

There is no date. Mandatory tagging of the sustainability statement is suspended by Directive (EU) 2026/470, in force 18 March 2026, until the ESEF Delegated Regulation is amended to cover it — which requires ESMA technical standards that have not been drafted. Any vendor quoting you a resumption date is guessing.

Is my IFRS S2 materiality assessment enough?

No. ESRS reports a topic material to people and the environment or to the company; IFRS S2 covers only the second. The impact side has to be assessed separately, and every omitted topical standard needs a stated reason.

Is it really free, and where does my data go?

Free, no sign-up. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded — which is exactly why it can be used on live commercial figures before a vendor has been cleared.

On what this is not. It is a self-assessment tool, not a certification, and not assurance. It does not carry the EU Taxonomy technical screening criteria per activity per objective — you assert whether each gate is met and it records the assertion. It does not tag XBRL. Those limits are published in the product beside the comparison against Workiva, Position Green and the other platforms, rather than left for you to discover.
