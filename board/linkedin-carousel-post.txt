A bank's financed emissions can fall by a third without a single borrower cutting a tonne.

The attribution factor is exposure divided by the counterparty's value. For listed equity that value is EVIC — a market price. Valuations rise, the denominator rises, the reported figure falls. Nothing changed in the real economy.

I spent a while going through PCAF properly and wrote up what I found. The part I keep coming back to is this: no emission factor database can lift a portfolio above data quality score 3. The database supplies scores 3 to 5 by construction. Scores 1 and 2 only exist where the counterparty gave you the data.

Which means the problem most institutions are trying to buy their way out of can only be solved by talking to clients.
