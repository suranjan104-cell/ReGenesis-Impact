# Benchmark standard — Big 4 published research

Derived from analysis of Deloitte Insights, PwC (Global Investor Survey, CEO
Survey, *AASB S2 unpacked*), EY (Global Climate Action Barometer, Center for
Board Matters) and KPMG (Survey of Sustainability Reporting). These are the
rules this board is built to.

## Architecture

Canonical order, shared by all four firms:

```
Cover (title + series line + month/year)
Contents
Foreword / Introduction
Key findings          ← designed, near-wordless, 5–7 stats
Executive summary     ← 400–700 words of prose
Chapters, each opened by a divider
What this means       ← actions, addressed to the reader, no service names
About the research    ← methodology, INCLUDING the data cut-off date
Endnotes
About the firm / contacts   ← everything commercial lives here and nowhere else
```

Methodology never opens the document. It is demoted to the back, after the
argument has been made.

## Figures

- **"Figure", not "Exhibit."** Exhibit is McKinsey/BCG house style; all four
  Big 4 firms use Figure.
- The figure **title states the finding**; the neutral descriptor of what is
  plotted is demoted to a subtitle.
- Note lines (rounding, exclusions) sit **above** the source line.
- `Source: …` bottom-left, smallest type on the page, sentence case, ends in a
  period. Third-party data cited first, own analysis appended after a
  semicolon — never substituted for the primary source.
- Publication wordmark bottom-right of every figure, so a screenshotted chart
  still carries its attribution.
- The denominator is disclosed on every figure that has one.

## Voice

- **Impersonal third person in findings; first-person plural only in the
  foreword and methodology.** The grammatical subject is the entities, not the
  author. "Deloitte found that…" is suppressed; the finding is stated directly.
- The reader is **"companies", "entities", "boards", "reporters"** — not "you".
  Second person is confined to headings and questions.
- **Always state the denominator.** "of the G250", "22 first-wave Group 1
  reporters", "857 companies across 50 countries".
- **Fraction, then figure**: "less than half (41%)" — meaning before precision.
- **Direction of travel with its baseline**: "95%, rising from 80% in 2022".
- **"say" / "believe" for self-report; "reported" / "disclosed" for observed.**
- **Concessive structure** carries criticism: *[credit] + but + [the real
  finding]*. "Boards increasingly understand sustainability risks, but
  understanding alone is no longer enough."
- **Modals preserve the reader's authority**: "can assess", "should consider" —
  never "must".
- **Recommendations attach to a real cohort and a real date**: "practical steps
  for June 2026 reporters", not "organisations on their journey".
- Agentless passive is the polite form for criticism: "key information was
  sometimes obscured".
- Short declarative fragments break up dense analysis. *Effort has been
  significant.*

### The two tests

1. **A sentence earns its place if it could be false.** "Understanding alone is
   no longer enough" could be wrong, and is therefore informative.
   "Sustainability is a driver of innovation and transformation" could not be,
   and is therefore filler.
2. **Jargon that is a precise reference compresses; jargon that is a metaphor
   inflates.** "Double materiality", "Scope 3", "reasonable assurance" have
   definitions. "Journey", "unlock", "leverage", "paradigm", "turning point",
   "future-proof" do not. Banned outright.

## Humane, not promotional

- **Publish the finding that embarrasses your own audience** — PwC assures
  sustainability reporting and still published "94% of investors believe
  corporate reporting on sustainability performance contains unsupported
  claims."
- **Name the limits of your own instrument.** EY measured disclosure, not
  behaviour, and said so: *it may be the case that boards are more involved
  than companies are currently disclosing* — then converted that limitation
  into the recommendation.
- **Concede progress before criticising it.**
- **Acknowledge the work is hard, with specifics** — "immature internal systems
  and a dependence on external value-chain data" names a structural obstacle
  rather than a failure of will.
- **Report variance, not just averages** — "ranging from 1 to 12", "varied
  widely in structure".
- **Give credit for voluntary effort.**

## Self-promotion

- **Zero service mention before the endnotes.** The analytical body runs its
  full length without asking the reader to buy anything.
- The commercial section carries an **explicit, self-labelling heading** — "How
  EY can help", "About [firm]". The label is the firewall; never bury a pitch
  under a neutral heading.
- Roughly **one page of firm content per 30–60 pages of analysis.**
- The **"what this means" section is advice, not a pitch** — imperative, no
  service-line names. Earn the inference; don't make the claim.
- The real call to action is **a named human with an email address**, not a
  demo request.

## Design

- Brand colour is an **accent, ≤10% of any content page**. Full fields are
  reserved for cover, dividers and back cover.
- **One highlighted element per figure in brand colour; everything else in
  neutral grey.** (Deloitte Insights, via Pentagram: "a restrained color
  palette allows the green to stand out and ensures the reader is never
  overwhelmed with multiple insights.") Genuine categorical series — where
  colour encodes an entity — are the exception.
- Serif display + sans body is the PwC-legitimised route to looking
  differentiated without looking unserious.
- **A visible grid is an asset, not scaffolding to hide** — Deloitte Insights'
  system is explicitly "inspired by tax ledgers, evoking rigor, order and
  confidence."
- Headline statistics at 4–8× body size, grouped in threes or fives.
- No drop shadows, gradients, 3-D charts or chartjunk.
- Every published report carries a **traceable document ID**.

## Sourcing caveat on this benchmark

Both studies were assembled from search-result summaries: this environment's
network policy blocked direct fetch of every Big 4 domain. The architectural
and voice patterns are consistent across many independent summaries and are
reliable; individual verbatim quotations should be re-checked against the
source PDF before being quoted publicly.
