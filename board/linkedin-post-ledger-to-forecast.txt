A new briefing on transition planning and climate scenario analysis: what IFRS S2 and AASB S2 require, what four supervisory exercises found when they ran the analysis themselves, and where the first wave of Australian reporters fell short.

Selected findings:

— MAS has made transition planning a supervisory expectation for banks, insurers and asset managers from September 2027, with scenario results required to feed credit, underwriting and investment decisions.
— AASB S2 requires two scenarios, one at 1.5°C and one where warming well exceeds 2°C. IFRS S2 prescribes neither.
— The paper behind the physical-risk estimates in NGFS Phase V has been retracted. Resilience assessments built on those outputs have a material assumption to disclose.
— Around 70% of reported emissions in the ECB's stress test rested on proxies rather than counterparty data.
— IFRS S2 requires a transition plan to be disclosed only if an entity has one. Just under two-thirds of first-wave Australian reporters disclosed one anyway, and ASIC found those plans were not clearly linked to targets and actions.
— Reasonable assurance over all Australian climate disclosures applies from 1 July 2030. Scenario analysis and transition plans attract none in the first year.
